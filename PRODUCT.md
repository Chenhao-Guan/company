# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Overseas procurement managers, marine engineers, locomotive maintenance directors, and industrial equipment buyers — predominantly male, 35–60, in shipping, rail, and power generation industries. They evaluate suppliers on technical credibility, manufacturing precision, and supply reliability. They visit the site to verify capability before making contact.

## Product Purpose

Renhe (厦门仁合) supplies precision-engineered spare parts for marine and locomotive diesel engines — cylinder covers, pistons, cylinder liners, bearings, crankshafts, and connecting rods. The website must communicate deep technical authority and manufacturing excellence to overseas buyers, converting visitors into RFQ conversations. Success = a buyer lands on the homepage and immediately thinks "these people know what they're doing."

## Brand Personality

Precise. Authoritative. Unflinching.

This is a company that machines parts to micron tolerances for engines that run at sea for months. The site should feel like a clean engineering drawing — every element placed with intention, nothing decorative, nothing that doesn't serve the purpose of selling competence.

## Anti-references

- Generic SaaS landing pages with gradient hero text and floating particle effects
- Template-looking B2B sites with stock photos of handshakes and factories
- Blue-purple gradient AI-generated websites
- Anything with "modern" rounded cards and soft shadows everywhere
- Dashboard-style admin aesthetics on a marketing site

## Design Principles

1. **Precision is the aesthetic.** Clean lines, tight grids, monospace accents for technical data — the design itself should feel manufactured.
2. **Products speak first.** Technical specifications, materials, and application contexts are the hero content. Not slogans, not stock imagery.
3. **Engineered restraint.** No decorative animation. Motion serves function (revealing specs, navigating detail). White space is structural, not aesthetic.
4. **Technical credibility over warmth.** Trust comes from demonstrating expertise, not from friendly copy. Specs, tolerances, certifications, brand compatibilities — that's what earns the inquiry.
5. **Dark-mode engineering blueprint.** The visual language draws from technical drawings, CAD interfaces, and industrial instrumentation — not from consumer web design.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA minimum
- High contrast ratios on technical specification text (critical for readability)
- Reduced motion support — all animations must have static fallbacks
- Clear, legible typography for older professionals who may not have perfect vision
